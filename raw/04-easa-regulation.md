# EU Regulators Finalize Mandatory Certification Rules for Urban Delivery Drones

*Aviation Policy Weekly — June 11, 2026*

The European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA) has finalized new rules requiring all delivery drones operating over populated urban areas to undergo a formal type certification process before commercial operation, closing a regulatory gap that startups in the space had been operating under since 2023.

The new rules, which take effect January 2027, apply specifically to drones operating below 150 meters in cities with population density above 1,500 people per square kilometer — a category that includes central Berlin, where Fernweh Robotics has been running test flights. Suburban and exurban operators, including companies like Aerotote Systems that already fly in lower-density areas, face a lighter-touch registration process rather than full type certification.

Industry analysts say the rule change is a meaningful setback for urban-focused delivery drone startups that had assumed a longer runway before facing certification requirements. "Companies that bet on operating in a regulatory gray zone now have a hard deadline," said aviation policy analyst Greta Voss. "Anyone targeting a 2027 commercial launch in a dense city needs to have certification substantially underway right now, not 'in early conversations.'"

EASA said it would begin accepting formal certification applications from urban drone operators starting in Q3 2026.
