# Interview: Mira Kessler on Building Fernweh Robotics

*Founder Notes blog — April 19, 2026*

We sat down with Mira Kessler, founder and CEO of Fernweh Robotics, to talk about the company's origin and where it's headed after the Sparrow announcement.

**On starting Fernweh:** "I spent six years doing propulsion work for a satellite company before I started Fernweh in 2023. I kept thinking about how much dumber last-mile delivery is than orbital mechanics — bikes, vans, idling in traffic. Sparrow started as a weekend prototype."

**On the business model:** Fernweh doesn't sell drones directly. Instead it operates Sparrow as a delivery-as-a-service platform, charging partner pharmacies and grocery chains per delivery. Kessler says this is a deliberate contrast to competitors who sell hardware outright.

**On competition:** Kessler was asked directly about Aerotote Systems, widely seen as Fernweh's closest rival. "Aerotote is targeting suburban and exurban delivery with bigger, heavier drones. We're not really in the same lane — they're solving a different last-mile problem than we are." She declined to comment further on Aerotote's recent funding round.

**On team:** Fernweh has grown to 45 employees since the March announcement, with new hires concentrated in flight operations and regulatory affairs — a shift Kessler attributed to "wanting to be ahead of the certification conversation, not behind it."

**On timeline:** Kessler reiterated the planned 2027 commercial launch, but added that "certification timelines are the biggest variable we don't fully control."
